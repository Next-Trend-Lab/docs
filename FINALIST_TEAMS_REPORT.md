## Introduction

As Hub Lead, I'm presenting five finalist teams from NexTrend Hub, Lagos for top 3 local winners selection. This report documents our community-driven evaluation process and the teams advancing to final consideration.

Per CATS protocol, we present five finalists from which the global organizing team will select three local winners for prizes.

---

## The Evaluation Challenge

I faced a conflict of interest: leading team selection while also competing as a participant. Rather than compromise the program's integrity, we paused and let the community design a better process.

On December 29, 2025, we gathered virtually and collectively determined how to proceed fairly.

## How We Decided

The community made three key decisions through voting:

**1. Who Gets Evaluated**
- All teams received 72-hour notice and 40-minute grace period
- Only 6 of 11 teams joined the call
- Community voted to evaluate only teams present
- Decision honored participants who showed up

**2. What Data to Use**
- Use only preexisting data from Globe website
- Eliminates last-minute manipulation
- Exception: K33P's data predated Globe, so we used their README
- Unanimous community consent

**3. How to Eliminate Bias**
- Five AI models evaluate independently: ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, Grok, Perplexity
- Same prompt to all models based on CATS Regenerative Framework
- Scoring: 1 to 10 scale with justification
- Eliminates my conflict of interest and single-model bias

### Evaluation Prompt

```
You are a judge for the CATS Hackathon, focused on regenerative innovation for Africa's invisible economy. Evaluate each project based on the CATS Regenerative Framework:

Community First Validation (Highest Priority): Rank projects higher if they clearly demonstrate deep local problem understanding through ground truth research. Solutions should address real community needs, not assumed problems.

Regenerative vs. Extractive (Critical Factor): Prioritize projects that restore value to communities rather than extract it. The solution must create a sustainable economic or social loop. It should not just be "for profit"; it must restore value back to the users and/or the planet through their economic or social model.

African Context Design: Favor solutions designed for African realities, data constraints, infrastructure gaps, low-cost devices, and offline-first functionality.

Systems Thinking Over Features: Reward projects that build foundational infrastructure (trust layers, verification systems, identity frameworks) rather than standalone features.

Transparency & Accountability: Prefer solutions with immutable audit trails, public verification, and mechanisms that replace blind trust with verifiable evidence.

Rate each project on a scale of 1 to 10 and provide detailed justification for your score.
```

**Note on Ties:** Blockprint and Colossus initially tied in one model's scoring. We reran evaluation with an improved prompt requesting clear differentiation.

## Five Finalists for Top 3 Selection

### 1. Catalyst | Score: 9.50
**Trust Based Credit Profiling for Invisible Merchants**

40 million micro-merchants contribute 58% of Nigeria's GDP yet remain invisible to banks. Catalyst transforms informal market activity into verifiable credit profiles using minimal data points. Makes the invisible visible without changing cash-based habits. Creates economic inclusion pathways while building trust infrastructure.

---

### 2. OSWEB | Score: 8.76
**Farm-to-Finance Agricultural Data Platform**

Farmers can't access loans because they lack verifiable harvest records. OSWEB creates low-data agricultural documentation that banks can use for loan assessment. Designed for rural, low-bandwidth contexts. Breaks the financing barrier keeping farmers trapped in poverty cycles.

---

### 3. K33P | Score: 8.64
**Social Recovery for Crypto Self-Sovereignty**

Seed phrase loss destroys crypto holdings and keeps millions away from crypto entirely. K33P enables social recovery through trusted guardians holding encrypted shards. Preserves self-sovereignty while adding human safety net. Makes crypto accessible without punishing human error.

---

### 4. TechKR | Score: 8.10
**Marketplace for Invisible Talent**

Lagos has skilled workers everywhere, tailors, plumbers, hairdressers, welders, yet they remain unemployed because they're invisible. TechKR makes informal sector skills discoverable and verifiable through mobile marketplace. Builds verification infrastructure creating trust between workers and clients.

---

### 5. Blockprint | Score: 7.50
**Student Infrastructure Accountability Platform**

Students pay tuition yet live with broken infrastructure while complaints disappear. Blockprint documents problems with immutable blockchain records. Makes institutional failures publicly visible and permanent. Shifts power dynamics through transparency.

## Teams Not Evaluated

Five teams didn't join the December 29 evaluation call despite 72-hour notice and 40-minute grace: CivicChain, Ratify, Inclusive, Alpha, and Colossus.

This wasn't about project quality. The community voted to proceed with teams present to honor those who showed up. All 11 teams contributed valuable ground truth research and prototypes advancing our mission.

---

## Why This Process Matters

We implemented multiple safeguards:
- Five AI models eliminated single-model bias and my conflict of interest
- Preexisting data sources prevented manipulation
- Full transparency with community consent at every step
- Complete documentation of all decisions

This demonstrated that communities can self-organize around fairness even when facing complex challenges.

## Recommendation

These five teams now advance for top 3 local winners selection by the global organizing team. Each demonstrated strong alignment with the CATS Regenerative Framework through ground truth research, systems thinking, and commitment to building trust infrastructure for Africa's invisible economy.
